Privacy notice

MYMINI164 · last updated 28 September 2026

MYMINI164 is a personal project for cataloguing 1:64 scale diecast model cars. This notice describes what it stores, where, who can see it and what you can do about it. It describes the application as it is actually built, not as a template.

The short version. To use MYMINI164 you need an account, which means an email address and a password. Everything else it holds is what you choose to record about your own collection. A new collection is private and shows nobody anything. There is a community here, so other collectors can find your profile and follow you, and you decide whether any of them can see what is on your shelf. What you paid is never shown to anybody, whatever else you open up. There is no advertising, no analytics, no tracking and nothing is sold or shared with anyone.

Who is responsible

MYMINI164 is run by an individual, not a company, and is the data controller for everything described here. For anything in this notice, write to help@mybatsurvey.co.uk.

What is NOT stored

  • No payment details. MYMINI164 takes no money and has no payment system.
  • No address, telephone number, date of birth or any other identity document.
  • No analytics, no cookies for tracking, no advertising identifiers, no fingerprinting.
  • No record of which pages you looked at or how long you spent there.

Who can see what

All of this is enforced in the database itself, by row level security policies and by functions that check who is asking, rather than by what the page chooses to show. A request for something you are not entitled to is refused by the database whoever makes it, including from outside the application.

Your collection

Your collection has one setting, which starts at Private.

SettingWho can see what is on your shelf
Private (the default)You, and nobody else at all.
Friends onlyYou, and collectors whose friend request you accepted or who accepted yours. Following somebody does not make them a friend and does not open this.
PublicYou, and any signed-in member of the community. Never anyone who is not signed in, and never a search engine.

Your wishlist and your activity feed have their own settings, which work the same way and also start private.

What you paid

What you paid, where you bought it, the order reference and your notes are never sent to another collector under any setting. They are not part of what a friend sees when they look at your shelf, and no ranking or aggregate is built from them.

Your profile

Every active collector can be found by other signed-in collectors: your display name, your username if you set one, anything you write in your bio and the catalogues you list as favourites. This is how anybody follows anybody, and it does not depend on your collection setting. A private collection stays private while its owner is perfectly findable. Anyone can follow you without your permission; you can block them, which stops them seeing or reaching you in either direction.

Community rankings

The ordinary rankings - most releases, most catalogues, most badges, rarest shelf and so on - include every active collector by default, and you can leave them at any time in Privacy settings. They publish a total and never a contents: a collector with a private collection can stand in the table with a number beside their name, and nothing in their collection can be read by anyone. Because a ranking already publishes those totals, a collector taking part will also show the same totals on their profile card; leaving the rankings stops both.

Most Valuable Collections is different. It is off unless you deliberately turn it on, it uses the catalogue's market valuation rather than anything you paid, and it never shows a purchase price or a profit.

The administrator

An administrator can see the list of accounts and display names, which is needed to run the site, and can award the four honorary badges. An administrator cannot read your collection, your prices, your settings or your private messages of any kind. That is not a policy, it is what the database does: the check was tested from an administrator's own account and refused.

Aggregate figures

MYMINI164 shows how uncommon a release is among its members, and how many collectors follow a given catalogue. Those figures are counts of distinct accounts and never carry anybody's identity. Where a count is small enough that it could point at a person, the count is not sent to the browser at all: the suppression happens in the database, not on the screen.

What your browser fetches from elsewhere

These requests reveal your IP address to the companies concerned, as any web request does. None of them receives anything about you or your collection.

  • Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com) for the typeface.
  • Photograph hosts for pictures the catalogue does not carry itself: chiefly the manufacturers' and collectors' sites and the shops a release is listed at.
  • Supabase for your own data.

What is kept in your browser

MYMINI164 uses your browser's local storage rather than cookies. It holds your sign-in token, your settings, a marker naming which account those settings belong to, your profile picture if you set one, and any barcodes you have taught it. Signing out clears all of it. Nothing there is sent anywhere except your own settings, to your own account.

How long it is kept

For as long as you have an account. Delete your account and your profile, collection, settings, badges, privacy settings, follows, friendships, blocks, notifications, activity and ranking totals are deleted with it, immediately and for good. There is no archive copy and no undo.

Two things deliberately survive, with your name taken off them: a correction or a missing car you sent in stays in the catalogue queue with no account attached to it, since the point of it was to fix a shared catalogue; and a notification somebody else already received that mentions you keeps the notification, without you as its sender. Neither carries anything about your collection.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you can ask for a copy of your data, ask for it to be corrected, ask for it to be deleted, object to processing carried out under legitimate interests, or ask for your data in a portable form. The application does several of these itself: "Download my data" in Your Account exports everything you have recorded in a machine-readable file, "Delete my account" removes it, and the rankings - the one thing here that rests on legitimate interests - can be left with a single switch in Privacy settings. Anything you cannot do from inside the application, ask for at the address above. You may also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Children

MYMINI164 is not aimed at children and asks for no age. It should not be used by anyone under 13.
